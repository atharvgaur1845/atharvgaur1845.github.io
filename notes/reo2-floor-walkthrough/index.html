The floor moves with the probe

A walkthrough of a REO @ NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper on trivial baselines for satellite self-supervised learning

The question

Satellites image the same field every few days, and a crop is defined by how it changes over a season. Self-supervised objectives for satellite imagery are usually compared against each other. They are rarely compared against something trivial.

The project started as a comparison of one objective against the standard ones: predict the latent state of a location at a future acquisition from its past (temporal JEPA). The benchmark initially had no trivial floor. Adding two changed the conclusion.

Setup

  • Data: PASTIS crop segmentation, official 5-fold split, validation fold, 10 Sentinel-2 bands, 128 × 128 patches, series truncated to at most 32 acquisitions (PASTIS has 38 to 61) for every cell including the floors.
  • Encoder: a shared ViT over 8 × 8 patches (256 tokens, width 512) and a causal temporal transformer. A narrower predictor maps the past to the stop-gradient EMA target embedding of a future frame. A variance-covariance penalty on the context embedding prevents a constant solution.
  • Objectives compared: temporal JEPA, spatial JEPA, SimCLR, BYOL, MAE.
  • Two floors:
    • raw_features: no encoder, no parameters. The raw bands averaged over each 8 × 8 patch, then over time. A 10-dimensional composite.
    • random: the temporal architecture with weights never trained.
  • Matched: 100 epochs, per-step batch 12, effective batch 192, 15-epoch probe. Not matched: compute. Temporal JEPA uses 6.24 GPU-hours per seed against MAE’s 0.55.
  • Readouts: linear head, small convolutional head, parcel k-NN (k = 20). Encoders frozen.
  • Pre-registration: before running the floors, a timestamped rule: the claim holds only if temporal JEPA beats both floors and the margin over the larger floor exceeds the across-seed standard deviation, reported either way.

At this probe the pre-registered rule passes: temporal JEPA clears the raw-band floor by 4.78 mIoU, 3.7 across-seed standard deviations. Spatial JEPA is within half a standard deviation of a baseline with no encoder. MAE, BYOL and SimCLR sit below it.

The floor moves

The probe heads differ only in spatial receptive field. Receptive field 1 is a strict linear probe, 3 is the convolutional head above, and 5 and 9 add stacked 3 × 3 convolutions.

Receptive field raw floor random floor temporal JEPA margin over raw floor
1 4.76 ± 0.02 8.41 ± 0.15 15.32 ± 1.13 +10.56 ± 1.12
3 14.47 ± 0.01 11.15 ± 0.20 19.26 ± 1.30 +4.78 ± 1.30
5 20.24 ± 0.02 12.31 ± 0.78 20.09 ± 1.29 −0.15 ± 1.28
9 21.45 ± 0.14 8.62 ± 2.85 19.88 ± 1.39 −1.57 ± 1.28

The zero-parameter floor rises by a factor of 4.5. The encoder moves from 15.32 to 19.88. The margin falls monotonically and is gone by receptive field 5. The paper does not claim a reversal at 9: at 1.2 standard deviations the sign is not established. What is established is that the measured advantage over a baseline with no parameters disappears once the probe can mix over a 5 × 5 neighbourhood.

The pre-registered rule inherited the same blind spot. It fixed the convolutional readout before probe capacity was known to matter. Across the sweep it passes at receptive fields 1 and 3 and fails at 5 and 9.

What the margin is made of

  • Temporal axis, mostly not the future. A shuffled-time control (frames permuted, dates left in order) kept about 70% of the conv margin at seed 0: 18.38 against 20.03, with a 14.48 floor. Single seed.
  • The anti-collapse penalty is load-bearing. Without it, effective rank of the pooled embedding falls from 312.4 to 1.49 of 512 and conv mIoU drops from 20.03 to 8.68, below both floors. Single seed.
  • The representation does learn something temporal. Acquisition month decodes from frozen spatial features at 63.9% for temporal JEPA against 47.0% for spatial JEPA (chance 8.3%). It learns; it just does not add much over the raw bands once the probe can mix spatially.

How the paper changed

The project’s first draft was titled “Temporal Redundancy Breaks JEPA on Satellite Image Time Series” and argued that temporal prediction beats spatial masking. Two things moved it:

  1. Seeds 1 and 2 of the original matrix failed at step 0 with out-of-memory errors on a shared 48 GB GPU, so the reported spread could not be recomputed. The runs were redone.
  2. The floors were run as the first priority, because the pre-registration made the headline claim unassessable without them. Their result reframed the paper around probe capacity on 2026-09-02.

What it does not establish

  • n = 3 seeds (SimCLR and BYOL n = 2); the collapse ablation and shuffled-time control are single-seed.
  • Frozen probes on one dataset, truncated to 32 acquisitions, no fine-tuning.
  • The capacity sweep keeps a fixed 15-epoch probe budget, so capacity and ease of optimisation are not separated.
  • SimCLR uses the negative count its batch allows, smaller than usual tuning.

Recommendation from the paper

Report floors at every probe capacity used, and read a margin as a property of the (objective, probe) pair rather than of the objective.
